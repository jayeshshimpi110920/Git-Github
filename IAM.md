Identity and Access Management (IAM) is a framework of policies and technologies to ensure that the right users (identities) have the right access (permissions) to the right resources within an organization. It plays a critical role in cybersecurity by controlling and managing user identities and access to systems, networks, and data. Here's a breakdown of key IAM concepts:

### 1. **Identification**:
   - **Identity**: Establishing who the user or entity is. This can be a person, device, or application. Every identity in an IAM system is assigned a unique identifier, such as a username, email address, or system ID.
   
### 2. **Authentication**:
   - **Authentication**: The process of verifying the claimed identity. Common methods include passwords, biometrics, multi-factor authentication (MFA), and tokens.

### 3. **Authorization**:
   - **Authorization**: Once a user is authenticated, the IAM system determines what actions the user is allowed to perform (e.g., read, write, execute) based on their assigned roles and permissions.

### 4. **Access Control Models**:
   - **Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)**: Assigns permissions to users based on their roles within the organization.
   - **Attribute-Based Access Control (ABAC)**: Uses attributes (such as department, location, or job title) to determine access rights.
   - **Policy-Based Access Control (PBAC)**: Uses predefined policies to determine access based on specific conditions.
   
### 5. **Identity Federation**:
   - **Federation**: Allows users to access resources across different domains or organizations using a single identity, typically via protocols like SAML or OAuth.

### 6. **Single Sign-On (SSO)**:
   - **SSO**: A technology that allows users to authenticate once and access multiple applications or systems without needing to log in again.

### 7. **Privileged Access Management (PAM)**:
   - **PAM**: Focuses on managing and controlling access for privileged accounts (administrative or superuser accounts) to minimize security risks.

### 8. **Identity Lifecycle Management**:
   - **Provisioning and Deprovisioning**: Ensures that user accounts and permissions are created when needed and removed when no longer necessary.
   - **Self-Service Password Reset**: Allows users to reset their passwords without admin intervention.

### 9. **Identity Governance**:
   - **Governance**: Involves monitoring and auditing user access to ensure compliance with policies and regulations.

IAM systems, such as AWS IAM, Microsoft Azure AD, and Okta, help organizations enforce security policies, reduce the risk of unauthorized access, and comply with regulatory requirements.
